## Task 1: Sensitivity to Initial Centroids and k-means++
Because standard k-means converges strictly to local minima, initialization dictates clustering quality. On the 9-point test dataset, random initialization frequently placed multiple seeds into the same blob, causing bad local traps with SSE reaching 96.120 (mean SSE 38.513). In contrast, k-means++ selects each subsequent seed with probability proportional to squared distance $D(x)^2$ from already selected centroids; this geometrically repels new seeds from existing clusters, achieving the optimal SSE of 0.1200 across every trial. When a cluster becomes empty, our implementation preserves its previous centroid coordinates, avoiding numerical singularities while allowing future reassignment.

## Task 2: The Curse of Dimensionality
As dimension $d$ increases, distance contrast $\frac{\max - \min}{\min}$ plummets from 488.58 at $d=2$ down to 0.982 at $d=50$ (dropping below 1.0) and reaches 0.153 at $d=1000$. Mean distance grows strictly as $\sqrt{d / 6} \approx 0.4082 \sqrt{d}$, matching theoretical expectation (measured 12.909 vs theoretical 12.910 at $d=1000$). Increasing sample size $n$ from 1,000 to 2,000 points slightly increases contrast ($0.982 \to 1.022$) due to extreme value sampling in the tails, but the relative spread $\sigma / \mu$ remains invariant at $0.083$. This confirms that the curse is an intrinsic property of high-dimensional space rather than finite sample size. However, Week 3's LSH on Jaccard similarity is immune to this breakdown because text shingles lie on a highly sparse, structured manifold rather than uniform continuous cubes, and MinHash preserves discrete set overlap probabilities $P(h(A) = h(B)) = J(A, B)$.

## Task 3: Pruning Distance Calculations via Triangle Inequality
In naive k-means, computing all $n \times k$ pairwise distances each iteration is overwhelmingly redundant once clusters stabilize. By the triangle inequality, if point $p$'s upper bound distance to its current centroid $c_j$ satisfies $u(p) \le \frac{1}{2} \min_{j' \neq j} d(c_j, c_{j'})$, $p$ cannot possibly be closer to any alternative centroid $c_{j'}$, completely bypassing distance calculations for that point. Because $k = 12$ is tiny compared to $n = 20,000$, computing all $\binom{k}{2} = 66$ inter-centroid distances costs negligible work ($O(k^2) \ll O(n k)$). Tracking Elkan's upper and lower bounds cut distance computations by 96.1% (398,366 calls down from 10,320,000) with 0.0000% SSE difference, reducing runtime from 40.31 seconds to 5.98 seconds.
