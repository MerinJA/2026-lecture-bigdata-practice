## Task 1: Dead Ends and Spider Traps
Without damping and dead-end handling, PageRank fails in two fundamental ways: dead ends cause total rank to leak out to 0 (measured 0.0000), while spider traps act as absorbing sinks that trap over 95% of the graph's rank (measured 1.0000 on C+D). Uniform teleportation ($\beta = 0.85$) resolves spider traps by granting a random escape probability of $(1 - \beta)$, preserving non-trap ranks ($A$ and $B$ retain $\approx 0.0375$ and $0.0694$). Redistributing leaked rank from dead ends evenly across all nodes strictly conserves the total probability mass at 1.000000.

## Task 2: Convergence Dynamics
As $\beta \to 1$, convergence iterations increase monotonically (14 iterations at $\beta = 0.50$ to 24 at $\beta = 0.99$). Because the spectral radius of the transition matrix is bounded by $\beta$, error contracts at a rate of $\beta^k$; higher damping weakens error contraction. Scaling graph size from 1,200 to 6,000 nodes (5x increase) leaves iteration count virtually unchanged (20 vs. 22 iterations at $\beta = 0.85$) because the spectral gap $1 - \beta$ is independent of dimension $N$, while wall time scales linearly ($0.015s$ to $0.105s$, a 7x increase) due to $O(|E|)$ edge traversals per step. The top 6 nodes remain identical across all values of $\beta$, but nodes 7 and 8 swap positions between $\beta = 0.50$ and $\beta = 0.70$, proving that choice of $\beta$ materially alters the relative ranking of closely scored pages.

## Task 3: Sparse Representation and Teleportation
The dense matrix formulation stores $N^2 = 1,440,000$ floats for a 1,200-node graph where density is merely 0.408%, squandering memory on explicit zeros. In sparse PageRank, the teleport term $(1 - \beta)/n$ and the uniform dead-end distribution $\beta \cdot \text{dead\_sum} / n$ do not require dense matrix multiplication: because they contribute an identical scalar offset to every single node, we precompute a single scalar base $\text{base} = (1 - \beta)/n + \beta \sum_{v \in \text{dead}} r[v] / n$ and initialize the next rank vector with it in $O(N)$ time. Outgoing edges are then streamed directly from the adjacency list. This sparse implementation holds only two vectors of size $N$ ($2,400$ floats), achieving a 600.0x memory reduction and a 56.0x speedup while matching dense PageRank to within $1.18 \times 10^{-15}$.
